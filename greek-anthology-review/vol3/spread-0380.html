printed p. 381 · epigram

683.—ANONYMOUS

On Alpheus and Arethusa

ALPHEUS is a male water, Arethusa a female, and Love accomplished their marriage by mixing the waters.

printed p. 381 · epigram

684.—ANONYMOUS

On the Fountain on the Island Taphos

I AM the fountain Nychea, daughter of Ocean and Tethys, for so the Teleboae named me. I pour forth a bath for the Nymphs and health for mortals. It was Pterelas, the son of Ares, who placed me here.

printed p. 381 · epigram

685.—ANONYMOUS

On Camarina the Sicilian Lake

MOVE not Camarina, for it is best unmoved, lest, if thou move it, thou make the lesser greater.

printed p. 381 · epigram

686.—ANONYMOUS

On the Eastern Gate of Thessalonica

EXULT in thy heart, stranger, when thou seest above the gate the prefect Basil,³ destroyer of the valour of insolent Babylon and light of incorrupt justice. Thou goest to the place of good government, the mother of excellent sons. Thou hast no need to fear the barbarian or sodomites. The Spartan for a wall has his arms, and thou a royal statue (or the statue of Basil.)

Extracted notes

  1. [1] One of the Echinades islands at the mouth of the Adriatic. (after “Taphos”; english; p. 380)
  2. [2] The first line alone is elsewhere cited as the response of Apollo when the people of Camarina asked him if they should drain the marsh near their city. (after “Lake”; english; p. 380)
  3. [3] As the terms of the epigram suit the emperor Basil I., who conquered the Arabs in Mesopotamia and was celebrated as a legislator, it probably refers to him in spite of the title "Prefect" given him. (after “Basil”; english; p. 380)
  4. [4] i.e. the Arabs. The Greeks at the time charged the Oriental nations with this vice. There is no reference to measures for its suppression. (after “sodomites.”; english; p. 381)
