81.—ANTIPATER OF SIDON

On the Seven Sages

Of the seven sages Lindus bore thee, O Cleobulus, and the land of Sisyphus 4 says that Periander is hers. Mytilene bore Pittacus and fair Priene Bias, and Miletus Thales, best support of Justice, Sparta Chilon, and Attica Solon—all guardians of admirable Prudence.

printed p. 51 · epigram

82.—ANONYMOUS

On Epicharmus

I HOLD Sicilian Epicharmus, a man armed by the Doric Muse for the service of Bacchus and the Satyrs.

printed p. 51 · epigram

83.—ANONYMOUS

On Thales

IONIAN Miletus nourished and revealed this Thales, first in wisdom of all astronomers.

printed p. 51 · epigram

84.—ANONYMOUS

On the Same

SMALL is the tomb, but see how the fame of the deep thinker Thales reaches to the heavens.

printed p. 51 · epigram

85.—DIOGENES LAERTIUS

On the Same

ONCE, Zeus the Sun, didst thou carry off from the stadion, as he was viewing the games, Thales the sage. I praise thee for taking him away to be near thee, for in truth the old man could no longer see the stars from earth.

Extracted notes

  1. [1] Nos. 83-133 are all derived from Diogenes Laertius' Lives of the Philosophers. Those of his own composition are not only very poor work (perhaps the worst verses ever published), but are often unintelligible apart from the silly anecdotes to which they refer. These I give in such cases in the briefest possible form. (after “83.”; english; p. 50)
  2. [2] Thales died from the effect of heat and thirst while watching the games. (after “earth.”; english; p. 51)
